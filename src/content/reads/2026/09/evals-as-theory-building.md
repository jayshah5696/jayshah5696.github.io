I liked the insistence that the evaluator itself needs to be attacked: in one six-hour campaign, an automated adversary found 89 escapes in 153 attempts, and the revised harness blocked all 89 on replay while detecting 49 planted defects. That gives a practical mental model for eval work: keep the attacks, cases, outputs, and replay results together so a score becomes a bounded, checkable claim rather than a dashboard number.
