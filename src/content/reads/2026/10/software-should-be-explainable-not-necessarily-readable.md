Most advice treats human readability as a requirement; this argues for code a model can explain to a person instead. The deliberately obfuscated Haskell sorting function makes the distinction vivid, and the claim that types provide "back pressure" gives the idea a mechanism: compiler errors help agents catch mistakes. I'm not sold on the broader case for agent-first language design yet; the author says there are no results to show, and the migration stories are anecdotes.
